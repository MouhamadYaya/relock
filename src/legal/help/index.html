1. Before you begin

Relock runs on iPhone only. The blocking itself is performed by Apple's Family Controls framework, which exists on iOS and has no equivalent Relock can use elsewhere.

One permission does all the work: Screen Time access. Without it Relock can display your rules but cannot enforce a single one. You are asked for it during onboarding, and you can check it at any time in Settings → Protection → Screen Time access. If it reads anything other than Granted, tap it and accept the iOS prompt.

Apple shows the permission sheet only a limited number of times. If tapping it no longer produces a prompt, grant it from the iPhone's own Settings app, under Screen Time, then reopen Relock.

2. The three kinds of block

A rule combines the apps you pick with one of three mechanisms. You choose the mechanism when you create the rule, and it is what makes Relock a friction tool rather than an on/off switch.

TypeWhat it doesBest for
Progressive delay A waiting screen appears before the app opens, and it gets longer with each opening in the same period. The reflex opening — the one you did not decide to make.
Time windows The apps are unavailable during hours you set, for example 10pm–8am. Protecting sleep, mornings, or working hours.
Daily open limit A maximum number of openings per day. Past the limit, the app stops opening until tomorrow. Keeping an app in your life without letting it take the day.

Rules live in Settings → Protection → My blocks, and on the Blocks tab. You can run several at once; the strictest one applying at that moment wins.

3. The pause

When a progressive delay triggers, Relock does not simply refuse. It asks "What are you really looking for?" and counts down. At the end you can still choose Open anyway — that is the point. The pause is there to turn an automatic gesture into a decision, not to win an argument with you.

The ambient sound during that screen can be turned off in Settings → Personalization → Pause ritual sound, and the vibrations in Vibration.

4. Strict mode

A rule marked strict cannot be stopped before it ends. No pause, no Open anyway, no editing the rule to shorten it. Relock will tell you plainly: this block cannot be stopped before it ends; that is exactly what you chose when you turned it on.

Enable it only when you want your future self to have no say. The one door that remains is the emergency unlock, and it costs you the rule itself.

5. A block is not applying

Almost every report we get comes down to one of these.

What you seeWhyFix
Blocks tab says every app is available, although you created rules. Screen Time access was refused or revoked, so iOS is not enforcing anything. Settings → Protection → Screen Time access, then grant it. Restart Relock afterwards.
One app opens normally while the others are blocked. That app is not in the rule's selection. Apple hands Relock opaque tokens, not app names — a category can miss an app you assumed was inside it. Edit the rule and add the app explicitly rather than relying on its category.
The rule shows a start time in the future. Nothing is wrong — a time-window rule only applies inside its window. Check the status line under the rule; it states when it starts.
Blocking stopped after you reinstalled or changed iPhone. Screen Time authorization is per-device and does not travel with your account. Grant Screen Time access again on the new device; your rules come back with your account.
Blocking works, but the app looks stuck on the shield screen. The iOS shield is a separate system extension; it can stay on screen a moment after a rule ends. Close the app from the app switcher and reopen it.

If none of these match, send us the app version from Settings → About — the Report a bug row fills it in for you.

6. Emergency unlock

Settings → Sensitive zone → Emergency unlock lifts everything immediately: no breathing, no waiting, no strict mode. It exists for real emergencies — an interview, a trip, a medical surprise.

It deletes your rules, it does not suspend them. You will have to build them again afterwards. That price is deliberate: it is what keeps the escape hatch from becoming the habit, and it is also our guarantee that Relock never holds your phone hostage.

7. Uninstall protection

When it is on and a block is active, iOS refuses to delete apps — including Relock. This closes the most obvious bypass: uninstalling the referee mid-match.

To remove Relock, first end or delete your active blocks, or use the emergency unlock, then delete the app normally.

8. Score and streak

Your global score is computed on your iPhone, from measurements Apple never hands to Relock. If it reads Calculating or says there is not enough measured data, it is because a score needs at least one full measured day — create a block and it arrives the next day.

The streak counts consecutive days in control. It is a mirror, not a scoreboard: losing it costs you nothing but the number.

9. Notifications

Relock sends two kinds: reminders (a streak at risk, a return after time away) and progress (weekly report and milestones). Each can be turned off separately in Settings → Notifications.

We never send promotional notifications. Note that once you deny notifications at the iOS level, only the iPhone's own Settings app can restore them — Relock cannot ask again.

10. Subscription and billing

Relock Pro is billed by Apple through your App Store account. Settings → Subscription → Manage my subscription opens the customer centre for plan changes and cancellation.

Changed phone, reinstalled, or signed in again and Pro is missing? Use Restore purchases in the same section. If it reports no purchase to restore, check that you are signed in to the same Apple Account that paid.

Cancelling has to happen at Apple. Deleting the app, deleting your Relock account, or signing out does not stop the billing — only your App Store account settings can. Do that first, or you will keep being charged.

11. Your data

Settings → Privacy → Export my data produces a JSON file containing your profile, your rules and your history. Settings → Account → Delete my account erases your profile, your rules and your whole history, on our servers and on this iPhone, with no way back.

Your usage measurements never leave your iPhone: Apple does not hand them to Relock, and only an aggregate score is computed on device. Crash reports carry your Relock account identifier, so that one person’s crash can be told from another’s, but never your email address. When a recording of the screen is attached to a crash, every piece of text, every image and every icon is replaced by a grey shape on your iPhone before anything is sent. You can switch crash reports off entirely in Settings → Privacy → Crash reports. The Privacy Policy is the authoritative description.

Deleting your account does not cancel your subscription — see section 10.

12. Still stuck

Write to contact@getrelock.com. From inside the app, Settings → Support → Report a bug pre-fills your version, build and language, which saves a round trip.

Useful things to include: what you expected, what happened instead, the rule type involved, and whether Screen Time access reads Granted.
